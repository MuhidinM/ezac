This is a comprehensive and detailed guide on the rules of Zakat, structured by asset type. It covers the rulings, calculations, reasons, and supporting evidence from the Quran and Sunnah.

---

# 📖 COMPREHENSIVE ZAKAT GUIDE

## 1. General Principles of Zakat

**Ruling:** Zakat is _Fard_ (an absolute obligation) and the third pillar of Islam.
**Reason:** The word "Zakat" means both "purification" and "growth." It purifies a Muslim’s wealth from greed, cleanses the soul, and redistributes wealth to the needy to ensure social justice.
**Conditions for Zakat to be Obligatory:**

1. **Muslim, Sane, Adult:** (Though Zakat is also due on the wealth of minors/insane, paid by their guardians).
2. **Full Ownership:** You must fully own and have access to the wealth.
3. **Nisab (Minimum Threshold):** The wealth must reach a specific minimum amount.
4. **Hawl (Lunar Year):** One full Islamic lunar year (approx. 354 days) must pass while the wealth remains above the Nisab. _(Exception: Agricultural produce and discovered treasures)._

**The Recipients (Who can receive Zakat):**
Outlined in **Quran 9:60**: The poor (_Fuqara_), the needy (_Masakin_), Zakat administrators, those whose hearts are to be reconciled, freeing captives/slaves, those in crippling debt, in the cause of Allah, and the stranded traveler.

---

## 2. Gold, Silver, and Fiat Money (Cash)

This applies to physical cash, bank balances, and precious metals kept as wealth or excessive jewelry.

- **Nisab for Gold:** 85 grams (approx. 3 ounces).
- **Nisab for Silver:** 595 grams (approx. 21 ounces).
- **Nisab for Cash:** Scholars mostly agree that the Nisab for cash should be pegged to the value of **Silver** (as it is currently much lower than gold, which benefits the poor by bringing more people into the paying bracket).
- **Calculation Rate:** **2.5%** (or 1/40th) of the total value.

**Ruling & Details:**

- You calculate the total value of your cash, gold, and silver on your Zakat anniversary date. If it equals or exceeds the Nisab, you pay 2.5% of the **entire amount**.
- _Jewelry:_ The majority of scholars (Hanafi) state Zakat is due on gold/silver jewelry whether worn or kept in a safe. Some scholars (Shafi'i, Hanbali) exempt jewelry that is worn daily, but paying it is considered safer.

**Evidence (Hadith):**

> Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) said: _"No Zakat is due on gold until it reaches twenty dinars [85g]. Once it reaches twenty dinars, half a dinar is due on it. And there is no Zakat on silver until it reaches two hundred dirhams [595g]. Once it reaches two hundred dirhams, five dirhams are due on it."_ (Sunan Abu Dawud)

## 3. Business Goods and Trading Assets (Urood al-Tijarah)

This includes any items bought with the absolute intention of reselling for profit (inventory, raw materials, land for resale, retail stock).

- **Nisab:** Equivalent to the Nisab of Gold or Silver.
- **Calculation Rate:** **2.5%**.

**Ruling & Details:**
To calculate business Zakat, use the formula:
**Total Cash + Value of Inventory (at current market/wholesale price) + Good Debts owed to you - Short-term Business Liabilities/Debts = Zakatable Amount.**

- _Note:_ There is NO Zakat on business fixtures, machinery, delivery vehicles, or buildings used for the business. Zakat is only on the _trading inventory_ and cash.

**Evidence (Hadith):**

> Samurah bin Jundub (RA) reported: _"The Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) used to command us to pay Zakat from what we prepared for sale."_ (Sunan Abu Dawud)

## 4. Modern Wealth: Real Estate, Shares, and Pensions

Because these did not exist in the exact modern form during the Prophet's time, scholars have analogized them using traditional fiqh.

**A. Real Estate & Property:**

- **Personal Home:** No Zakat on the house you live in.
- **Rental Property:** No Zakat on the total value of the property. Zakat is only due on the **rental income** at 2.5%, if the money is saved and reaches the Nisab and Hawl.
- **Flipping/Selling:** If you buy property strictly to sell it, it is a business good. Pay 2.5% on the total market value of the property every year.

**B. Stocks and Shares:**

- **Trading (Short-term):** If you actively buy and sell stocks, they are trading goods. Pay 2.5% on their total market value on your Zakat date.
- **Investing (Long-term Dividends):** If you hold shares for dividends, you do not pay Zakat on the market value of the share. Instead, you pay Zakat on the _zakatable assets_ of the company (cash + inventory). Many Islamic charities provide calculators for company balance sheets, or historically, some scholars apply a flat 2.5% on the dividends earned.

**C. Pensions / 401(k):**

- If you have full access and control over the funds, you pay 2.5% annually.
- If it is locked and you cannot withdraw it without severe penalty, scholars say you pay Zakat for one year only when you finally withdraw it.

**Evidence:**
Based on the general Quranic injunction to pay from "what you have earned" (Quran 2:267).

## 5. Agricultural Produce (Zakat al-Zuru' wa al-Thimar)

This applies to crops that are cultivated, measured, and can be stored (like wheat, barley, dates, and raisins). Most scholars do not include highly perishable vegetables (like tomatoes or cucumbers).

- **Nisab:** 5 _Wasqs_, which is approximately **653 Kilograms** (or 1,440 lbs).
- **Calculation Rate:**
  - **10% (Ushr):** If the land is naturally irrigated (rain, rivers, springs) without cost.
  - **5%:** If the land is artificially irrigated (using purchased water, pumps, well machinery) at a cost to the farmer.
  - **7.5%:** If half naturally and half artificially irrigated.
- **Hawl:** NO Hawl is required. Zakat is due on the day of harvest. If you harvest twice a year, you pay twice a year.

**Reason:** The rate fluctuates based on the farmer's hardship. If it costs more to grow, Islam lowers the Zakat rate to 5%.

**Evidence (Hadith):**

> Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) said: _"No Zakat is due on less than five Wasqs of dates or grains."_ (Sahih Muslim)
> And he (ﷺ) said: _"On that which is irrigated by the heavens (rain), rivers, and springs, a tenth (10%) is due; and on that irrigated by well water (artificial means), half a tenth (5%) is due."_ (Sahih Al-Bukhari)

## 6. Livestock and Animals (Zakat al-An'am)

Zakat is due on specific types of livestock: Camels, Cows/Buffaloes, and Sheep/Goats.
**Conditions:**

1.  They must be _Sa’imah_ (freely grazing on public land for more than half the year). If you buy feed for them for most of the year, they are exempt from animal Zakat (though they may fall under business Zakat if you sell them).
2.  They are kept for milk, breeding, or fattening, NOT as working animals (like oxen used for plowing).

**Calculations (Brief breakdown):**

- **Sheep & Goats (Nisab = 40):**
  - 1 to 39: No Zakat
  - 40 to 120: 1 Sheep
  - 121 to 200: 2 Sheep
  - 201 to 300: 3 Sheep (Then 1 extra sheep for every 100 thereafter).

- **Cows & Buffaloes (Nisab = 30):**
  - 1 to 29: No Zakat
  - 30 to 39: One 1-year-old calf (_Tabi'_)
  - 40 to 59: One 2-year-old cow (_Musinnah_)
  - 60: Two 1-year-old calves

- **Camels (Nisab = 5):**
  - 1 to 4: No Zakat
  - 5 to 9: 1 Sheep
  - 10 to 14: 2 Sheep
  - 15 to 19: 3 Sheep
  - 20 to 24: 4 Sheep
  - 25 to 35: 1 one-year-old female camel (_Bint Makhad_). (The scale gets highly specific from here upward).

**Evidence (Hadith):**

> Found in the detailed, written instructions given by Abu Bakr (RA) to Anas bin Malik regarding the obligations of Zakat as prescribed by the Prophet (ﷺ). (Sahih Al-Bukhari 1454).

## 7. Minerals and Buried Treasure (Rikaz & Ma'din)

- **Rikaz (Buried Treasure):** Valuables buried in the earth from pre-Islamic times (like an ancient chest of gold).
  - **Rate:** **20% (Khums)**.
  - **Ruling:** Payable immediately upon discovery. No Nisab and no Hawl required.
- **Ma'din (Mined Minerals):** Extracted resources like gold, silver, oil, or gems.
  - **Rate:** **2.5%** (According to the majority of scholars) once it reaches the Nisab equivalent of gold/silver.

**Evidence (Hadith):**

> Abu Huraira reported that the Prophet (ﷺ) said: _"In buried treasure (Rikaz), a fifth (20%) is due."_ (Sahih Al-Bukhari & Muslim)

---

## 8. Debts (Owed to you vs. Owed by you)

- **Debts Owed TO You (Receivables):**
  - _Good Debt:_ If you are highly confident the borrower will pay you back, you must include this money in your annual Zakat calculation.
  - _Bad/Doubtful Debt:_ If you are unsure you will ever see the money again, you do not pay Zakat on it yearly. If it is eventually returned to you, you pay Zakat on it for _one year only_ (according to the Maliki school, which is widely adopted today).
- **Debts Owed BY You (Liabilities):**
  - You can deduct immediate, short-term debts (due within the lunar year) from your zakatable wealth before calculating.
  - _Long-term debts (like mortgages or student loans):_ Do not deduct the entire 30-year mortgage from your wealth, as this would mean you never pay Zakat. You may only deduct the _next 12 months_ of required payments.

## 9. Zakat al-Fitr (Sadaqat al-Fitr)

Unlike annual Zakat, which is a tax on _wealth_, Zakat al-Fitr is a tax on the _individual (headcount)_.

**Reason:** To purify the fasting person from idle talk and obscene behavior during Ramadan, and to ensure the poor have food to enjoy the day of Eid.
**Ruling:** Obligatory on every Muslim—male, female, child, elderly, and newborn (if born before sunset on the last day of Ramadan)—provided they have food exceeding their family's needs for the day and night of Eid. The head of the household pays on behalf of their dependents.

- **Time of Payment:** Must be paid during Ramadan and strictly **before the Eid prayer**. If paid after the Eid prayer, it is merely considered ordinary charity (Sadaqah).
- **Amount / Calculation:** **One _Sa'_ (approx. 2.5 to 3 Kilograms)** of staple food common to the region (such as wheat, barley, dates, rice, corn, etc.).
- **Paying in Cash:** The majority of classical scholars (Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali) insist it must be paid in food. However, the Hanafi school (and many modern scholars and Islamic councils) permit giving the equivalent value in cash, as it is often more useful for the poor to buy what they specifically need today.

**Evidence (Hadith):**

> Ibn 'Umar (RA) said: _"The Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) enjoined Zakat-ul-Fitr on the one who fasts (i.e. Muslims) to purify the fasting person from indecent words or actions, and to provide food for the needy. It is one Sa' of dates or one Sa' of barley on every slave and free person, male and female, young and old among the Muslims."_ (Sahih Al-Bukhari)
> Ibn Abbas said: _"Whoever pays it before the [Eid] prayer, it is an accepted Zakat [al-Fitr], and whoever pays it after the prayer, it is ordinary charity."_ (Sunan Abu Dawud)

### Summary Checklist for Your Zakat Anniversary:

1. Identify your Islamic lunar date (e.g., 1st of Ramadan).
2. Tally cash on hand and in banks.
3. Tally current value of Gold/Silver.
4. Tally current market value of Business Inventory & shares.
5. Add reliable debts owed to you.
6. Subtract immediate debts you owe for the year.
7. If the total is > Nisab (approx. value of 595g of silver), multiply the total by **0.025 (2.5%)** and distribute it to eligible recipients.
